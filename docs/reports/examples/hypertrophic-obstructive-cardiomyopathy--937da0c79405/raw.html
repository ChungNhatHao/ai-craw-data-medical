Hypertrophic obstructive cardiomyopathy

  • Info
  • Life/DD/TPD
  • IP
  • Health
I42.1 * I42.2
Hypertrophic cardiomyopathy * HOCM * HCM

Hypertrophic cardiomyopathy is the most common inherited cardiomyopathy

Causes

This is inherited usually in an autosomal dominant fashion. There are many different genetic mutations that cause this condition and the spectrum of disease severity may be high.

Symptoms

Palpitations or episodes of syncope or faintness may signify potentially life-threatening arrhythmias. Symptoms of heart failure such as breathlessness may occur.

A history of sudden death in the family history especially at a young age is an important factor in risk stratification.

Complications

Arrhythmias, heart failure and sudden death.

Treatment

Betablockers are used to reduce the heart rate. Restriction of the exertion of the individual is advised to keep the pulse below 120 beats per minute. An implantable defibrillator may be used to prevent or reduce the risk of Ventricular Fibrillation. Ablation of the muscle bulk in the interventricular septum may reduce muscle mass but the risk of arrhythmias remain.

Prognosis

There is a wide range of severity and each case deserves careful assessment. The prognosis depends on the ventricular wall dimensions, the degree of outflow obstruction as measured by the gradient within the left ventricle, the occurrence of any arrhythmias and any reported sudden death in the family history.

Disability

May occur with heart failure or the development of arrhythmias

Supportive evidence

Cardiological report with follow-up

Cardiac CT / MRI or coronary angiography (invasive)

Echocardiography (transthoracic or transoesophageal)

Resting ECG

Exercise ECG

Blood pressure
