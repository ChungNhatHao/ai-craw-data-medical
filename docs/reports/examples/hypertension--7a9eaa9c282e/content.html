Hypertension

The elevation of the systolic and/or the diastolic blood pressure. Hypertension is one of the classic risk factors for vascular disease. Persistent hypertension can result in complications in various organs. Additional risk factors (hyperlipidaemia, diabetes mellitus, smoking, overweight) increase the probability of complications. Hypertension can be essential or secondary.

Causes

Primary, essential or idiopathic hypertension: Various factors appear to play a role: Heredity, environmental factors (salt intake, obesity, occupation, family size, crowding), metabolic disturbances. Secondary hypertension: Renal disorders (renal hypertension), hormonal disturbances (endocrine hypertension e.g. Cushing’s syndrome), coarctation of the aorta.

Degree of severity

Blood pressure readings

Retinal changes (Keith, Wagener and Barker classification)

Increasingly home measurements are taken or a 24-hour blood pressure monitor recording performed. How these are interpreted is important as they will be different to readings performed at a Doctor’s clinic. The readings will be lower in the vast majority of cases so different levels are used to judge severity.

Symptoms

Primary hypertension is usually asymptomatic unless severe. It is common for those diagnosed to relate symptoms of headaches, dizziness, fatigue, epistaxis, nervousness to the elevated blood pressure.

Complications
  • Heart: Left ventricular hypertrophy and cardiac failure, Myocardial infarction.
  • Eyes: Retinopathy (retinal haemorrhages, exudates, papilloedema).
  • Kidneys: Renal insufficiency which may progress to total renal failure.
  • Aorta, peripheral vessels: Aortic aneurysm, arterial occlusive disease
  • Brain: Cerebrovascular accident.

Treatment

Medication with primary hypertension. With secondary hypertension, medication; medical or surgical treatment of underlying cause.

Prognosis

Depending on cause, degree of severity, response to treatment and complications. Favourable prognosis for successful treatment before onset of complications.

Disability

Hypertension is asymptomatic and can usually be well controlled with drugs. It should not cause disability in the absence of complications such as kidney disease, myocardial infarction, cardiac failure or cerebrovascular disease.
