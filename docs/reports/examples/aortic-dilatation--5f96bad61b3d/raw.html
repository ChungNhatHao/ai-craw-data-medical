Aortic dilatation

Abdominal aortic aneurysm * Aortic dilatation * Aortic dissection * Ascending aortic aneurysm * Dilatation ascending aorta * Dissecting aneurysm * Mycotic aneurysm * Peripheral aortic aneurysm * Peripheral vascular aneurysm * Thoracic aortic aneurysm * Thoracoabdominal aneurysm

Aortic dilatation refers to the abnormal enlargement of the aorta, the largest blood vessel in the body. Aortic dissection is a serious condition where a tear occurs in the inner layer of the aorta.

The aorta has distinct sections: aortic root, ascending aorta, aortic arch, descending thoracic aorta and abdominal aorta.

Causes

Presence of hypertension, obesity, hyperlipidemia, connective tissue disorders, atherosclerosis, advanced age. About 20% are hereditary.

Symptoms

Symptoms may include back pain, cough, shortness of breath, chest pain. However, most cases are asymptomatic until dissection occurs.

Treatment

Medication: beta blockers, angiotensin receptor blockers, statins. Surgery and/or procedures: open chest surgery, aortic root surgery, valve aortic root repair

Prognosis

Dilatations smaller than 4 cm are generally considered low-risk for rupture. They can be monitored annually without immediate intervention. Dilatations over 4 cm require close monitoring and, in certain cases, intervention to prevent complications such as dissection or rupture. The prognosis is worse if the dilatation is over 4.5 cm [1, 2, 3]. Hereditary aortic disease is associated with greater risk including rate of progression.

Supportive evidence

Cardiological report with follow-up

Cardiac CT / MRI or coronary angiography (invasive)

Echocardiography (transthoracic or transoesophageal)

Resting ECG

Blood pressure
