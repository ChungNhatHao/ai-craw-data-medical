# Atrial fibrillation

AF * Absolute arrhythmia * Tachyarrhythmia absoluta * Holiday heart syndrome * Chronic atrial fibrillation

Atrial fibrillation (AF) is characterised by disorganised atrial activity. When an abnormally high atrial rate develops, irregular transmission of stimuli to the ventricles occurs resulting in a rapid, irregular heart rate. AF is a common cardiac rhythm disturbance. It may occur as a brief symptom- free episode with transient bouts of irregular heart beat or as a sustained form of arrhythmia (paroxysmal AF or transient AF). Episodes of AF may spontaneously revert to normal sinus rhythm or may enter a chronic stage. Although AF may present as a transient episode in otherwise fit subjects it usually is an indication of associated cardiac disease.

| **Causes** | Organic heart damage (coronary artery disease, mitral stenosis or incompetence, inflammatory and degenerative changes in the heart muscle, hyperthyroidism or toxic effects of alcohol excess). In some cases no cause is found (i.e. lone AF) It may be seen in normal subjects, particularly during emotional stress or following surgery, exercise, or acute alcoholic intoxication. |
| **Symptoms** | Palpitations, nausea, weakness, lightheadedness, irregular pulse. AF may be present in the absence of symptoms. |
| **Complications** | Episodes of AF, even of short duration are associated with a predisposition to pulmonary and peripheral embolism and an increased incidence of embolic stroke. |
| **Treatment** | Anti-arrhythmic drug therapy, electrical cardioversion or radio-frequency ablation for sinus rhythm to be restored. |
| **Prognosis** | Depending on cause. |
| **Disability** | May occur. The morbidity associated with AF is related to excessive ventricular rate, which may lead to hypotension, pulmonary congestion or angina pectoris; the pause following cessation of AF, which can cause syncope; systemic embolisation, which occurs most commonly in patients with rheumatic heart disease; loss of the contribution of atrial contraction to cardiac output, which may cause fatigue; and anxiety secondary to palpitations. If on chronic anticoagulation therapy, restriction from working with dangerous machinery, working at heights or hazardous work may be considered. |
