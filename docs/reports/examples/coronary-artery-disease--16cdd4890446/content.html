Coronary artery disease

Coronary artery disease (CAD) is the pathological process of plaque accumulation causing narrowing of the coronary arteries (stenosis). The disease course is chronic and usually progressive, but modifiable by control of risk factors and preventive treatment.

Severe narrowing of arteries can lead to an inadequate blood supply to part of the heart and cause an imbalance between the demand of the muscles for oxygen and the supply by the coronary arteries (ischaemia). Stenoses can also progress to a total occlusion of a coronary artery (heart attack).

In non-obstructive CAD, the major risks are slow progression to obstructive disease and sudden occurrence of plaque rupture, consequent formation of blood clots in the artery and subsequent further narrowing or total occlusion.

Causes

Multiple factors play a role for the occurrence and progression of CAD, including high levels of blood lipids (especially LDL cholesterol), high blood pressure, smoking, and diabetes. Narrowing of the coronary arteries can be caused by plaque formation, coronary artery spasms, inflammation of the coronary artery, severe anaemia, and arterial blood clots.

Symptoms

Non-obstructive coronary artery disease can be silent, i.e. symptom-free. With increasing narrowing of the coronary arteries symptoms such as chest pain or discomfort, shortness of breath, nausea, and dizziness can occur.

In severe disease, angina pectoris is a typical symptom. Angina pectoris is a tightness or choking sensation, a squeezing, pressing type of chest pain usually of short duration. The pain often radiates to the left shoulder and both arms. It can also radiate to the back, neck, jaw, teeth, and below the ribs. Symptoms are often triggered by physical or mental stress, as this increases the demand of the heart muscle for oxygen. Symptoms are usually relieved at rest. With very severe disease, symptoms can also be present at rest.

Complications

Heart attack, sudden death, arrhythmias.

Treatment

Depending on disease severity: Control of underlying cardiovascular risk factors, pharmacotherapy (ASA/Aspirin, Betablockers, ACE inhibitors, calcium channel blockers, statins), coronary artery bypass surgery, percutaneous transluminal balloon angioplasty (PTCA).

Prognosis

The prognosis depends on several factors: degree of stenosis of the coronary arteries, affected vessels, underlying risk factors, modification of these risk factors after diagnosis, the degree of left ventricular hypertrophy and any associated rhythm abnormalities, and therapy [1-3].

The standard coronary risk factors (blood lipids, blood pressure, smoking, BMI, blood sugar / diabetes) are important for the assessment of the risk of disease progression [4].

Supportive evidence

Cardiological report with follow-up

Cardiac CT / MRI or coronary angiography (invasive)

Echocardiography (transthoracic or transoesophageal)
