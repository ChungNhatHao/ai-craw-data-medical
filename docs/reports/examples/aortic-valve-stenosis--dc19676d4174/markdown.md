# Aortic valve stenosis

I35.0 * Q23.0

Aortic stenosis

Narrowing of the aortic valve, often combined with aortic incompetence. The left ventricle has to force blood through the narrowed aortic valve causing hypertrophy and later dilatation of the left ventricle.

| Column 1 | Column 2 |
| --- | --- |
| **Causes** | Congenital (e.g. bicuspid aortic valve), rheumatic fever, degenerative (atherosclerotic). |
| **Symptoms** | Attacks of dizziness, angina pectoris on effort, exertional dyspnoea, syncope. |
| **Complications** | Left ventricular failure, endocarditis. |
| **Treatment** | Medical treatment: avoidance of strenuous physical activity, diuretics, digitalis / Surgical treatment – there are various procedures: / Valve replacement with a mechanical or tissue prosthesis. / Valve reconstruction/repair, rare and usually restricted to post-traumatic injury. / TAVI (transcatheter percutaneous aortic valve implantation), an alternative therapy for high risk patients, involves a synthetic valve being transported to the heart through the femoral artery. |
| **Prognosis** | Depending on severity of stenosis; after surgery depending on the extent of left ventricular failure [1, 2, 3]. |
| **Disability** | Individuals who exhibit symptoms of aortic stenosis often need to limit their activity; a transfer to modified duties may be necessary. As symptoms arise, valve replacement is often considered. |
