# Pulmonary hypertension

I27.0 * I27.2 * I27.8

Primary pulmonary hypertension * PPH * Secondary pulmonary hypertension * Idiopathic pulmonary hypertension

Hypertension in the pulmonary circuit; may be primary idiopathic, or secondary to pulmonary or cardiac disease. In this condition pressure is increased in the pulmonary arteries. This causes increased resistance to the blood flow in the pulmonary arterial circulation.

| --- | --- |
| Causes | Idiopathic pulmonary hypertension: Increased pulmonary artery pressure and pulmonary vascular resistance without an obvious cause. A genetic basis for primary pulmonary hypertension appears likely. Familial primary pulmonary hypertension occurs in approximately 7% of cases, the pattern of inheritance identified as autosomal dominant. / Secondary pulmonary hypertension: Secondary pulmonary hypertension can result from left sided cardiac disease, low oxygen levels, sleep apnoea, thromboembolic diseases and lung disease, e.g. COPD and Interstitial lung disease. It can also arise from intracardiac shunts in congenital heart disease. / Risk factors that have been linked to the development of unexplained pulmonary hypertension include essential hypertension, human immunodeficiency virus, portal hypertension, collagen vascular disease, and congenital shunts resulting in increased pulmonary blood flow. |
| Symptoms | The most common symptoms of PPH are shortness of breath with exertion and general fatigue. Syncope, dizziness, ankle or leg swelling, and chest pain or pre-cordial pressure are often observed during the course of the disease. |
| Complications | Right ventricular failure, reduced life expectancy. |
| Treatment | Thromboendarterectomy, Sildenafil intravenous prostacyclin, high-dose calcium blockers, anticoagulation, diuresis, lung transplantation. |
| Prognosis | The course of PPH is often one of steady deterioration and reduced life expectancy; non-transplanted patients have a one year survival of 68 - 77%, and a five year survival of 22 - 38%. |
| Disability | Primary pulmonary hypertension has no cure. Untreated, it leads to right-sided heart failure and death. |
