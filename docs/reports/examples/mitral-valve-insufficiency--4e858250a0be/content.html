Mitral valve insufficiency

I34.0 * Q23.3
Mitral incompetence * Mitral regurgitation * Mitral valve insufficiency

The result of deformity of the leaflets or the annulus (ring) of the mitral valve. Normally this valve is closed during systole. An incompetent valve allows blood to reflux into the left atrium during systole. Mitral insufficiency may cause left ventricular dilatation, which in turn causes more incompetence.

Causes

Rheumatic fever, infective endocarditis, endomyocardial fibrosis, mitral valve prolapse.

Symptoms

Recurring symptoms such as syncope, dizziness, shortness of breath, and/or chest pain linked to heart valve insufficiency - tend to emerge primarily in advanced stages of the condition, indicating more significant cardiac impairment.

Complications

Left ventricular hypertrophy, heart failure, atrial fibrillation.

Treatment

Surgical or catheter-based valve replacement, repair or reconstruction (mitral annuloplasty).

Prognosis

Depending on extent of cardiac damage and response to treatment [1, 2, 3]. Severe mitral valve insufficiency in symptomatic individuals carries a poor prognosis if left untreated, often leading to progressive heart failure and reduced life expectancy. [1, 2, 3].

Disability

Although rare in asymptomatic individuals, recurrent symptoms associated with heart valve insufficiency - such as syncope, dizziness, shortness of breath, and chest pain - can significantly reduce a patient’s physical capacity and hinder everyday activities, including walking long distances, climbing stairs, and sustaining employment.

Supportive evidence

Cardiological or medical report with follow-up

Echocardiography (transthoracic or transoesophageal)

Resting ECG
